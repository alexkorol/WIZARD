# Wave 05 source audit — `amulet_bronze_capsule`

## Verdict

**REJECT the current row and source pairing; do not generate.**

British Museum 1872,0604.643 is not one bronze capsule pendant. It is an
Etruscan necklace assembly of **nine gold bullae** on one chain. One bulla is
embossed with a river god, three with a youth, and five are plain. The complete
necklace and all of its suspension construction are directly visible in the
official views.

The row therefore fails on material, object count, construction, suspension, and
roster need. Isolating one plain gold bulla could be a defensible component study,
but translating it into bronze, assigning the necklace's complete chain to that
one component, and calling its interior visibly hollow are not supported by this
record. The roster already contains four other capsule/bulla rows, including a
dedicated gold bulla.

This is an audit-only rejection. No prompt was authored and no image was
generated.

- Material role: **gold only**. The record does not support bronze or copper

- Plain-bulla shape role: **component reference only**. The plain members support

- Hollow-body role: **not explicitly recorded or visibly open**. “Bulla” and the

## What the source actually supports

For the necklace as a complete object:

- one fine gold chain loop
- nine substantial bullae distributed around the lower loop
- eight rounded/double-convex pendants plus one triangular central pendant
- broad rolled gold suspension tubes threaded directly on the shared chain
- several figured embossed faces and five plain faces
- terminal, clasp, and bead details at the chain closure

For one plain bulla considered only as a component:

- gold sheet/embossed construction
- rounded to slightly oval, double-convex body
- average size approximately 45 x 35 x 12 mm
- broad multi-roll or divided tubular suspension fitting across the top
- plain gold face with a faint perimeter seam/border

The exact source does **not** support:

- bronze
- one isolated pendant as the registered complete object
- a dedicated full chain loop for one isolated pendant
- a visible open cavity
- a sturdy cast capsule
- one simple single rolled tube
- a blank oval bronze body

## Necklace extraction and cord/loop decision

Character or necklace visibility does not require converting every visible
pendant into a separate item. Here, the registered object itself is an ensemble.

- A complete loop is source-observed only for the **nine-pendant necklace**.
- The full chain cannot be reassigned to one extracted bulla without changing the
  construction and object count.
- Rendering the source faithfully as a necklace fails the row's single-object
  requirement and produces nine competing focal objects.
- Rendering one bulla on that full chain invents a new assembly.
- Rendering one bulla pendant-only would remove the row's requested chain but
  still leave the unsupported bronze translation and heavy bulla-family overlap.

Therefore neither full-chain nor pendant-only framing rescues this source for
`amulet_bronze_capsule`. The row should be cut or given a different exact source
for one complete bronze capsule.

## `S` / `1x1` legibility

- The full nine-bulla necklace is too complex for `1x1`; individual pendants
  become repeated gold dots and the central triangular bulla competes with the
  round members.
- One isolated plain bulla would be legible as a rounded gold medallion/capsule,
  but that is no longer the exact registered assembly.
- A front view hides whether the body is hollow; a three-quarter view shows
  convexity and tube depth but still reads as an ordinary round bulla.
- The proposed bronze oval would enter the already crowded circular/oval
  medallion neighborhood represented by current and post-calibration amulets.
- The source has no 64 px feature that uniquely distinguishes a bronze capsule
  after gold, ensemble arrangement, and embossed figures are removed.

## Failure taxonomy

- **Wrong material:** source is gold; row says bronze.
- **Wrong object count:** source is nine bullae plus chain; row says one pendant.
- **Assembly extraction:** one necklace component is treated as the registered
  complete object.
- **Unsupported chain reassignment:** the shared necklace chain is presented as
  one pendant's dedicated full loop.
- **Unsupported hollow visibility:** no open cavity is visible and the catalogue
  does not explicitly say hollow.
- **Tube simplification:** source shows broad divided/multi-roll top fittings,
  not one generic rolled tube.
- **Defining-context erasure:** removing eight bullae and all embossed members
  discards the source's ensemble construction.
- **Material-swap base:** changing gold to bronze manufactures a progression rung
  rather than translating observed construction.
- **Family saturation:** four other planned bullae/capsules already cover D,
  keel, round, copper, gold, and exotic-material variants.
- **Icon-scale ensemble collapse:** nine pendants cannot remain distinct at
  `1x1`.
- **Motif/copy hazard:** retaining youth or river-god faces would introduce
  source-specific figured decoration; removing them leaves a generic round bulla.

## Disposition

Do not generate `amulet_bronze_capsule` from BM 1872,0604.643. Do not extract one
plain pendant merely because it is visible in the necklace. Either cut the row as
redundant or replace it with an exact ancient source for **one complete bronze or
copper-alloy capsule pendant** whose hollow construction, suspension interface,
and standalone object status are directly documented. Any replacement must also
prove a silhouette distinct from the four bullae already in the roster.

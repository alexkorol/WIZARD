# Wave 05 source audit — `amulet_copper_cowrie`

## Verdict

**HOLD the current row; viable after factual correction and one framing choice.**

WITT.434 is a strong, compact pendant source whose cowrie-shaped metal body,
long central slit, and suspension hardware remain readable at `1x1`. It earns a
different silhouette from the natural oval shell, stone drops, bullae, discs,
and seal pendants in the roster. It is not ready to generate under the current
row wording:

- the British Museum records **bronze**, not copper
- the attribution is **Roman (?)**, not securely dated Roman or pre-Roman
- the catalogue does not record a casting technique
- the photographs show a small pierced top lug and a **separate** large
  open-ended suspension ring, not one unambiguously integral top loop
- no historical neck cord survives; the white string in both photographs is
  museum handling/display material

Recommended disposition is an intentional pendant-only base with no cord
fragments. Correct the name/material to bronze or copper alloy, describe the
small pierced lug accurately, and do not claim a secure cast technique or date.
No prompt was authored and no image was generated.

## Observed construction

Directly observed and safe to lock after material correction:

- one complete, compact bronze/copper-alloy cowrie-form pendant
- length approximately 38.1 mm
- elongated, slightly tapering oval body with strongly convex shell volume
- one deep narrow longitudinal slit/groove occupying most of the front
- a rounded, nearly plain reverse
- one narrow neck above the shell body
- one small complete pierced lug/eye at the top of that neck
- one separate large round-section metal suspension ring passed through the
  small lug; the ring is visibly open-ended in the official views
- dark brown metal with substantial green copper-alloy patina
- no extra charms, beads, gemstone, inlay, inscription, rays, or decorative
  frame

Do not lock:

- pure copper
- a specifically documented casting process
- a fused/integral large suspension loop
- a historical cord, chain, clasp, or necklace length
- a pristine symmetrical natural-shell surface

The metal body appears complete. The small pierced lug also appears complete.
The large separate ring is source-observed but open-ended; the record does not
clarify whether that gap is original construction, later deformation, or damage.
It should not be fused into the body or described as integral.

## Cord decision

**Intentionally omit the full cord for this base.**

The July 24 correction permits either a complete wearable loop or a clearly
intentional pendant-only object with no cropped cord fragments. WITT.434 already
has strong, visible suspension hardware and a distinctive pendant silhouette.
A full neck cord would be a general historical inference, not exact-source
evidence, and would consume much of the `1x1` frame.

The white string in the official photographs is a modern museum support/handling
cord tied through the separate ring. It is not a source for color, material,
knot, length, or wear configuration. Do not copy it and do not leave two cord
ends cut by the frame.

If a later portfolio decision requires all amulets on necklaces, use one complete
plain dark fiber or leather loop from a separate general construction reference,
keep it secondary behind the pendant, and explicitly record that role. The
preferred exact-source treatment remains pendant-only.

## Failure taxonomy and hazards

- **Material mismatch:** current ID/name/description say copper; exact source
  says bronze.
- **Date overstatement:** Roman attribution is questioned and no absolute date is
  given.
- **Technique overstatement:** “cast” is not listed for the exact object.
- **Hardware fusion:** generator may merge the small pierced lug and separate
  large ring into one fantasy loop.
- **Damage copying:** large ring's open gap may be accidental damage and should
  not become a dramatic broken hook.
- **Museum-fixture contamination:** white cord and blue backdrop are
  documentation artifacts, not historical construction.
- **Cord crop/tangle:** a full neck loop at `1x1` can shrink the pendant or create
  cut ends, coils, and lasso forms.
- **Natural-shell drift:** avoid cream nacre, ribs, a shell hinge, or realistic
  marine-shell gloss; this object is oxidized bronze.
- **Anatomical exaggeration:** although the catalogue discusses vulval
  simulation, retain the bounded cowrie-shell geometry and do not add literal
  anatomy, gore, or erotic detailing.
- **Charm-cluster drift:** no tooth, beads, second shell, tassels, fringe, or
  dangling side charms.
- **Patina overcorrection:** preserve readable bronze beneath restrained green
  oxidation; do not render a featureless corroded lump.

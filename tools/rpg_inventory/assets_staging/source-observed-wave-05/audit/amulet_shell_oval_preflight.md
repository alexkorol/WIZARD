## Source-observed construction

Safe exact-source locks:

- one natural shell only
- compact rounded-oval body, approximately 4 × 3.6 cm
- strongly convex, hollow shell volume rather than a flat disc or stone
- cream and warm tan body with muted pink-brown natural growth bands
- broad natural aperture with a thick smooth lip occupying much of one face
- one large, irregular suspension perforation through the upper side wall
- modest natural shell sheen, not gemstone or lacquer gloss
- naturally asymmetric outline and surface pattern
- no metal setting, cap, bail, wire wrap, beads, symbols, fringe, tassel, or
  second charm

The exterior view shows the perforation open through the shell wall. Its edge
is irregular and worn but visually continuous; there is no evidence of a
modern grommet or added suspension fitting. The aperture view confirms the
hollow shell body and a substantially complete thick lip. Small edge
irregularities, grime, dark residue, and archaeological discoloration are not
design features.

Do not infer:

- a species or modern common shell name
- a drilled-hole technique more specific than a worn suspension perforation
- a historical cord, knot, necklace length, beads, or fastening
- deliberate symbolic marks from the dark natural bands
- polishing, lacquering, inlay, carving, or metal reinforcement

## Cord and framing decision

**Pendant-only; omit the cord entirely.**

The exact source supports the standalone pendant identity and the complete
suspension perforation. It does not support a cord. The complete-loop rule
allows an intentional pendant component with no cord fragments; it does not
justify inventing a wearable loop or showing two clipped ends.

For `S` / `1x1`, show one isolated shell in a slight three-quarter orientation
that exposes both the irregular side perforation and enough of the broad
aperture/lip to establish shell identity. Keep roughly 10–12% safe margin and
let the shell dominate the frame. A straight exterior-only view risks reading
as a drilled pebble or egg; an aperture-only view hides the suspension hole.

Clean the presentation to active-service readability while preserving natural
banding, modest shell sheen, worn perforation geometry, and asymmetry. Remove
museum background, floating support illusion, burial dirt, and distracting
damage.

## Failure hazards

- adding a full cord unsupported by the exact source
- cropped cord, floating cord ends, tangled coils, or a cord that does not pass
  through the perforation
- adding a tooth, second shell, bead row, fringe, charm cluster, or character
  costume context
- calling the shell a cowrie or assigning any unsupported species
- turning the broad aperture into the bronze cowrie's narrow central slit
- flattening the shell into a disc, cabochon, bead, pebble, or calcite drop
- metal bezel, cap, bail, grommet, wire wrap, chain, or precious setting
- carved eye, rune, spiral, face, sigil, inscription, or deliberate dark symbol
- nacre rainbow, pearl finish, gemstone sparkle, lacquer, or modern jewelry
  polish
- idealizing the suspension hole into a small perfectly circular machine bore
- copying burial grime, severe chips, black residue, museum gray background,
  catalogue presentation, or floating artifact shadow
- over-enlarging the aperture until the object reads as a vessel, mouth, mask,
  or creature

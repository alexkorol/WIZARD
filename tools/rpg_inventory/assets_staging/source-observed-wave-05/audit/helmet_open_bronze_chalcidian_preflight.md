## Construction locks

- Empty, wearable, bronze-led helmet; no head, face, skin, hair, ears,
  mannequin, or flesh-colored interior filler.
- One continuous high, deep, rounded bowl with its full rear volume visibly
  present.
- Close molded/hammered brow transition, open eye field, and a restrained
  central nasal. Do not turn the opening into a closed Corinthian mask.
- Two long shaped cheekpieces with mechanically matching opposite-side
  construction. Either the Met hinged variant or a fixed integral variant is
  valid, but do not hybridize the two.
- Deep open ear cutouts between cheekpieces and rear bowl.
- Short continuous, mildly everted/flared rear neck edge spanning the back.
  “Pronounced” must not become an Imperial Gallic plate or medieval tail.
- Dark, empty interior and a narrow functional lining edge at most.
- Plain active-service bronze: warm healthy metal, hand-working, restrained
  handling polish, shallow scratches, edge wear, and mild localized tarnish.
- Three-quarter hero view must prove the dome/back, near cheek, far structural
  counterpart, ear opening, facial opening, nasal, and neck edge.

## Hard exclusions

- No crest, plume, horsehair, crest socket, top knob, horns, wings, spikes, or
  fantasy face.
- No medieval spangen construction, segmented plates, crossbands, nasal-bar
  great-helm language, mail, articulated medieval tail, or modern fasteners.
- No snake-head pins, animal terminals, engraved scenes, symbols, script,
  embossed scrolls, pasted discs, gems, colored inlay, decorative leather
  stripes, or unsupported ornament.
- Do not copy green verdigris, black corrosion crust, powdery oxidation,
  burial staining, pitting, encrustation, cracks, holes, missing metal,
  deformation, museum labels, stands, or flat catalogue lighting.
- Do not omit the hidden cheek counterpart or reduce the rear to a flat
  facade. Perspective may conceal area, not construction.

## S 2x2 framing

The primary Met object's 24.1 x 19.7 x 24.8 cm dimensions form a compact,
roughly square three-dimensional mass. S canvas and 2x2 runtime footprint are
appropriate.

Use a slightly elevated three-quarter angle with enough side margin for the
leaf cheek and enough rear margin for the neck edge. Keep the dome apex,
nasal tip, both cheek endpoints, and rear lip inside frame. A flat front view
risks a backless facade; a flat side view risks losing the opposite cheek and
eye/nasal identity.

## Failure taxonomy

- **Prior-art duplicate:** existing balanced art already occupies the
  Chalcidian silhouette.
- **Misassignment risk:** that art is presently labeled Illyrian despite
  stronger Chalcidian geometry.
- **Structural-counterpart failure:** one missing cheek, hinge, or rear half.
- **Corinthian collapse:** eye/nasal opening closes into a full face mask.
- **Attic collapse:** crown becomes shallow and brow becomes a broad visor.
- **Pilos/simple-bowl collapse:** cheek, ear, nasal, or neck structures vanish.
- **Late-medieval drift:** spangen plates, crossbands, later nasal helm, mail,
  or articulated tail.
- **Source averaging:** mixing fixed and hinged cheek construction, or copying
  object-specific snake pins/incised decoration.
- **Museum-surface copying:** archaeological corrosion, holes, cracks,
  deformation, and labels become intentional design.
- **Fantasy escalation:** plume, crest, animal face, horns, gems, or pasted
  ornament.
- **Framing failure:** cropped dome/back, nasal, cheek tip, or rear lip.

# Wave 05 replacement-source preflight:
# `preparation_bronze_grinder` / BM 1976,0501.505–506

## Decision

**HOLD; do not generate or regenerate.**

British Museum 1976,0501.505–506 is a genuine complete associated
cosmetic-grinder set and fixes the fatal incompleteness of the former
1977,0403.1 source. The museum catalogs 1976,0501.505 as the pestle and
1976,0501.506 as the mortar. They are two sequential registration numbers,
not one accession number, but both come from King Harry Lane Cemetery burial
203, share find code SB/DH and Jackson 2010 catalogue number 436, and are
explicitly described as a set. The museum also states that the pestle fits
snugly in the mortar groove.

The lead cannot yet pass the exact-source visual gate. The two official object
pages expose image records 821342001 and 1281178001, but the official raster
downloads were access-denied in this environment and no connected browser was
available. The catalog also supplies no dimensions. Without an inspected
official view, exact overall curvature, piece-to-piece scale, resting
orientation, terminal silhouettes, and S 1x1 readability remain unverified.
The row must not move to generation from catalog prose alone.

Hold reasons:

- **official views not inspected:** exact silhouette and visible condition
  remain unverified;
- **no published dimensions on the object records:** S 1x1 scale/readability
  cannot be measured for this exact set;
- **registration correction required:** the set is 1976,0501.505 **and**
  1976,0501.506, not a single 1976,0501.505 accession;
- **material correction required:** use catalog material **copper alloy**;
  "bronze" appears in the pestle description but is not a verified alloy
  analysis in the accessible record;
- **framing still unproved:** "fits snugly" confirms the working interface,
  not necessarily the visually best nested storage pose for an S icon.

No prompt was prepared and no image generation or regeneration was performed.

## Catalog-locked construction and mechanics

Mortar:

- end-looped;
- broad elliptical bow, quite low;
- plain convex walls;
- partially flattened keel;
- capacious broad V-section longitudinal groove;
- off-centre basal wear slot;
- bulbous knobbed terminal with neat neck moulding;
- slender ring-like loop, slightly out of alignment with the bow.

Pestle:

- end-looped;
- slender circular-section rod;
- plumpest toward an upturned tip;
- tip turns slightly to one side;
- wear facet on the convex working face;
- circular ring-like loop and simple neck moulding mirroring the mortar loop;
- fits snugly in the mortar groove.

Mechanical interpretation:

- the convex pestle face bears against the broad V groove;
- longitudinal rocking/shearing concentrates contact at the pestle wear facet
  and the mortar's off-centre basal wear slot;
- the mirrored end loops associate the two parts and allow suspension;
- the components are not hinged, latched, boxed, or mounted to a backing.

The set is cataloged as plain and has no described animal or human figure.
That makes it much safer than 1977,0403.1 for the existing row, but "plain"
must not erase the knobbed terminal, paired ring loops, simple neck mouldings,
wear features, or mild axial asymmetry.

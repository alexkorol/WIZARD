## Candidate

Working name: **New Kingdom Cartonnage Quiver**

Primary object: Metropolitan Museum of Art 21.2.5 / object 554470, a Dynasty 18
Egyptian quiver dated ca. 1550–1295 B.C. and catalogued as leather over linen
cartonnage.

This is roster-worthy in principle because its tall, flat-sided case silhouette
and cartonnage-backed construction are materially and visually distinct from the
accepted cylindrical rawhide quiver, the held woven quiver, the slatted organic
tubes, and the combined gorytoi already present in reviewed art and targets.

It remains on HOLD because the only exact official photograph does not prove
three mandatory output facts: a visibly open and empty mouth, the rear harness
or strap, and the joins that attach that carrying system. Those features may not
be invented.

## Mandatory source gate

| Required evidence | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Complete body | PASS | Exact Met photograph shows the full tall case and rounded base. |
| Open, empty mouth | FAIL / unresolved | Upper edge is damaged, but the cavity and interior are not visible. |
| Base | PASS | Rounded closed foot is clearly visible. |
| Back/harness attachment | FAIL / unresolved | Only a front/oblique face is supplied; no carrying strap or attachment is proven. |
| Joins | PARTIAL | Surface bands and layer seams are visible, but body assembly and harness joins are not fully established. |
| No arrows/bow/lid | PASS in source view | None appears in the exact artifact photograph. |
| No invented fittings | PASS only if held | No metal fitting is visible or catalogued; unresolved organic harness parts cannot be added. |

Because every mandatory construction element must be evidenced before a brief,
the concept cannot proceed to prompt or generation.

## Construction locks if the hold is cleared

- Leather over linen cartonnage is the entire material thesis.
- Tall, narrow, flat-sided or shallow rectangular case; not a round tube.
- Rounded closed foot/base, not a copper shoe or metal cap.
- Organic brown leather surface with restrained surviving painted/worn areas.
- Preserve only the applied horizontal bands/seams and U-shaped front feature
  actually visible in the source.
- No reed, cane, bark tube, bundled wood slats, rawhide cylinder, basketry, or
  lacquer substitution.
- No bronze, brass, copper, iron, rivets, buckles, collars, rim bands, base cups,
  gems, or prestige garnish.
- No arrows, shafts, fletching, bow, bow limb, bowstring, secondary bow case, or
  combined gorytus silhouette.
- No lid, cap, flap, or flush closure.
- Do not repair the damaged lip into a speculative original profile.
- Do not interpret the U-shaped applied feature as a handle or suspension loop
  unless a credible source explicitly proves that function.
- Do not invent a back strip, harness, strap path, stitch pattern, or attachment
  tabs.

## Grid and canvas recommendation

If later evidence clears the hold:

- Canonical grid: **P 2×3**.
- Tall portrait canvas with the full object occupying most of the height.
- Near-front three-quarter angle only if the newly found source proves both the
  front details and the rear carrying attachment.
- The mouth must be tipped enough toward camera to show a dark, visibly empty
  interior while the rounded base and rear attachment remain readable.
- One standalone quiver only, isolated; no wearer, mannequin, arrows, bow, or
  accessory cluster.

The image should read first as a flat cartonnage-backed arrow container, not a
scabbard, shield board, coffin fragment, or cylindrical tube.

## Hazards

- Mistaking broken upper material for a deliberately open lip.
- Inventing a dark cavity to satisfy the open-empty rule.
- Treating the front U-shaped feature as a handle, strap loop, or harness anchor.
- Mirroring the photographed front into an invented rear construction.
- Adding a generic shoulder strap with invisible attachment points.
- Turning the body into an ordinary round rawhide or wood-slat tube.
- Adding Egyptian-looking metal trim, gold, painted ornament, or prestige
  hardware not shown by the source.
- Over-restoring damaged layers and seams.
- Making every item visible on an archer character board into a roster item.

## Release condition

Move from HOLD to PASS only after an exact museum/catalogue view, conservation
record, archaeological publication, or credible full reconstruction proves all
of the following for this object or a securely matching type:

1. an open mouth and empty interior;
2. the rear face;
3. the complete carrying strap or harness;
4. the attachment points and join method for that harness; and
5. the relationship of those parts to the rounded base and visible front
   features.

If that evidence cannot be found, retire this candidate rather than generating
a plausible reconstruction.

## Verdict

**REJECT as authored.**

British Museum 1986,0705.1 is a genuine Early Byzantine incense censer with
surviving suspension hardware and residue evidence. It does **not** have the
row's proposed construction. The exact object is an open, unperforated
copper-alloy bowl on three short feet. One long chain is attached between two
lugs. There is no lid, no perforated bowl, no three-chain suspension, and no
rigid crown.

The row's “perforated bowl under three taut short suspension chains meeting at
a rigid crown” is a different censer type assembled from generic expectations.
Correcting it to the exact single-chain tripod bowl would also duplicate the
`focus_bronze_censer` slot, which cites this same object and is already mapped
to a balanced ritual output. The source should not seed another invented
variation.

This is an audit-only rejection. No prompt was authored and no image was
generated.

## Exact construction locks

The catalogue and official photograph jointly establish:

- one open, lidless bowl;
- a deep hemispherical lower body;
- lower body constricting before a broad inverted/flared shoulder;
- a narrow in-turned upper rim;
- three short, sturdy, slightly splayed feet;
- two opposed suspension lugs at the rim/shoulder;
- one 360 mm copper-alloy chain joining those two lugs;
- twisted figure-of-eight attachment links at both ends;
- larger terminal links made from finer-gauge metal;
- the main chain also formed from twisted figure-of-eight links;
- restrained crudely drawn horizontal lines around the vessel;
- black burnt deposit confined to the interior;
- no lid, cover seat, central crown, handle, spout, pedestal, figures, or
  sacred relief.

The source does not show or describe perforations through the bowl. The only
openings are the functional suspension lugs and chain links. The broad dark
interior is the open mouth, not a missing perforated lid.

## Completeness, joins, and residue

### Surviving structure

- Bowl, three feet, two lugs, and one complete described chain survive.
- Each chain end connects to a lug through a twisted figure-of-eight link.
- The chain is approximately 4.1 times the bowl width, so it is neither three
  short arms nor a compact crown assembly.
- The object is stable as a standing tripod bowl and can also be lifted by its
  single loop chain.

### Damage

The museum records an old break crossing the shoulder to the right of one lug
and a corresponding break across the rim to the left of that lug. These breaks
must not be copied into active-service art.

### Soot/incense evidence

The black interior deposit was analysed by the British Museum Conservation
Research Section. It contains:

- ash;
- metal corrosion products;
- traces of gum resin, interpreted as likely incense such as myrrh or
  olibanum.

This is direct functional evidence for a censer. An active-service version may
retain a thin matte soot stain inside the bowl, but not loose ash, resin lumps,
charcoal, flame, smoke, glowing embers, or excavated corrosion.

## Chain-censer identity test

**Partial object-family PASS; row-specific FAIL.**

The chain-censer identity is source-observed in the broad sense: this is
catalogued as a censer and one suspension chain remains attached.

The proposed silhouette is not source-observed:

- actual: **one chain between two lugs**;
- row: **three chains**;
- actual: no central distribution fitting;
- row: rigid crown;
- actual: open unperforated bowl;
- row: perforated bowl;
- actual: three standing feet;
- row: feet omitted from the defining description.

Do not replace the exact single-loop suspension with the more familiar
three-chain thurible grammar. A three-arm suspension can be sourced from a
different complete ancient censer, but not from 1986,0705.1.

## Active-service finish

A source-faithful restored surface may show:

- healthy dark-gold to brown copper-alloy metal;
- shallow casting/hammering and drawn-line variation;
- light handling polish on rim, feet, lugs, and chain;
- mild localized brown tarnish;
- thin black soot only inside the open bowl;
- individually hand-twisted chain links with small variation.

Do not copy:

- broad green corrosion, mineral crust, burial staining, black external
  corrosion, pitting, flaking, cracks, rim/shoulder break, bent feet, or
  conservation repairs;
- modern brass polish, chrome-like reflections, perfect identical links, welded
  chain, stamped fittings, carabiners, or hardware-store chain.

## `S` / `2x2` framing gate

The **bowl itself** fits an S / 2x2 relic icon, but the exact chain creates a
composition problem:

- bowl width is 88 mm while chain length is 360 mm;
- laying the full chain beside the bowl creates a long loose loop and clutter;
- pulling it upward forms one tall inverted-U suspension, not three short
  chains or a crown;
- shortening it enough to fit tightly above the bowl changes an exact measured
  feature.

A faithful icon would need a three-quarter view with:

- all three feet or clear cues for the rear foot;
- both lugs;
- the entire single chain visible as one controlled high loop;
- open dark bowl and interior soot visible;
- no cropped links, dangling end, or extra suspension parts.

That construction could read as a small tripod incense bowl with a chain, but
it is not the compact crowned thurible promised by the row. The source's bowl
is only 8.8 cm wide, so “substantial” must come from icon readability rather
than false physical enlargement.

## Failure hazards

- **Three-chain invention:** one chain/two lugs replaced by three suspension
  arms.
- **Rigid-crown invention:** hook, cap, handle, distribution ring, or crown
  added where none survives.
- **Perforation invention:** solid open bowl pierced with smoke holes.
- **Lid invention:** domed cover, hinge, cap, perforated lid, or lid seat added.
- **Foot loss:** source's three sturdy feet omitted or hidden so the object
  becomes a generic hanging cup.
- **Tripod inflation:** extra feet, animal legs, tall stand, pedestal, or
  brazier base added.
- **Chain multiplication:** decorative side chains, long free ends, chain web,
  or loose sprawling hardware.
- **Gravity failure:** chain floats rigidly, links do not bear load, lugs do not
  align with tension, or bowl hangs at an impossible angle.
- **Fire/content invention:** flame, smoke, sparks, embers, charcoal, resin
  chunks, ash pile, incense cone, or glowing contents.
- **Sacred/costume drift:** crosses, saints, figures, shrine architecture,
  censers shaped as churches, text, sun/radial motifs, gems, or filigree.
- **Modern incense hardware:** tea-light cup, mesh insert, screw lid, stamped
  chain, swivel, split ring, carabiner, clip, or polished brass thurible.
- **Archaeology finish:** copied green corrosion, black crust, breaks, pits,
  bent metal, burial dirt, museum stand, label, or white sweep.
- **Bad framing:** cropped chain, duplicate vessel, front-flat view hiding
  feet/lugs, chain tangled across the bowl, hand, priest, room, altar, or
  floor/cast shadow.

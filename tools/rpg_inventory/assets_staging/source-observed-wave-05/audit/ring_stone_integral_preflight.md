# Wave 05 source audit — `ring_stone_integral`

## Verdict

**REJECT for generation from BM EA54799.**

EA54799 is a legitimate Eighteenth Dynasty stone ring fragment and the British
Museum explicitly calls its construction “integral.” It therefore supports an
original one-piece stone bezel/shoulder/hoop type. It does **not** preserve a
complete hoop: the catalogue identifies only the bezel and shoulder section and
marks the object incomplete. All three official views confirm that the hoop is
missing.

The proposed low **blank** bezel is also not source-observed. The surviving bezel
is a large elongated oval/rounded plaque whose entire visible face is occupied by
a royal cartouche and hieroglyphs. Removing that defining face leaves a generic
shouldered signet silhouette already covered by current, staged, promoted, and
planned rings. A complete blank stone ring generated from this source would be a
speculative reconstruction plus a material swap, not a distinct exact-source
base.

This is an audit-only rejection. No prompt was authored and no image was
generated.

## Exact source and source roles

- British Museum object record: EA54799 / 1920,0612.57  
  https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA54799
- Catalogue facts:
  - object type: finger-ring
  - culture/period: Eighteenth Dynasty
  - findspot: Egypt
  - material: stone; no more specific lithology is supplied
  - description: “The bezel and shoulder section of an integral stone
    finger-ring, inscribed with a cartouche containing hieroglyphs.”
  - bezel dimensions: 2.22 cm long x 1.19 cm wide
  - inscription position: bezel
  - inscription script: hieroglyphic
  - inscription subject: royal
  - condition: fair; incomplete
- Chronology gate: **PASS**. The Eighteenth Dynasty is securely ancient and far
  earlier than the through-AD-600 project ceiling.
- Material role: **primary but nonspecific**. The source supports stone, not a
  named stone variety or a uniform clean ivory-white finish.
- Construction role: **partial primary source**. “Integral stone finger-ring”
  supports the original single-material/single-piece construction type; the
  surviving artifact only supplies the bezel and shoulder fragment.
- Hoop role: **none**. No full hoop, underside opening, section, diameter, or
  closure survives in the official views.
- Surface/decoration role: **exclusion evidence**. The cartouche and
  hieroglyphic royal inscription define the observed face and cannot be converted
  into an allegedly source-observed blank panel.

## Observed geometry and construction limits

Directly observed:

- one elongated oval to softly rounded-rectangular stone bezel/face
- bezel dimensions 22.2 x 11.9 mm, approximately 1.87:1
- shallow domed face with a thick perimeter edge
- broad, low shoulder mass flowing away from the bezel
- surviving shoulder edges broken before forming a finger opening
- mottled gray, tan, and brown worn stone rather than clean uniform pale stone
- the face is densely occupied by a bounded cartouche/hieroglyphic composition

Supported only at the object-type level:

- the original ring was integral, so its lost hoop was intended as part of the
  same stone body rather than a separate metal shank or mounted gemstone

Not source-observed and therefore not construction locks:

- a complete closed hoop
- hoop thickness, cross-section, inside diameter, underside cutaway, or exact
  taper from shoulders into the hoop
- a blank bezel
- a recessed empty socket
- a detachable seal, gemstone, metal fitting, inlay, or setting

The source does not justify reconstructing the missing hoop by copying the hoop
geometry of another ring. A replacement exact source would be required before a
complete one-piece ring could pass the source-observed gate.

## Blank-bezel test

**Fail.** Blankness is not a conservative cleanup of incidental damage. The
royal cartouche and hieroglyphs occupy almost the entire visible bezel and provide
the object's identity. Erasing them removes the defining source-observed face,
while replacing them with lines, rays, pseudo-glyphs, or a generic geometric
panel would create a copied or invented symbol problem.

The low profile itself is observed, but a low blank face alone does not preserve
enough distinctive information to separate this object from the project's
existing blank-bezel/signature ring macros.

## `S` / `1x1` legibility

- A reconstructed complete ring could fit `1x1`, but the source does not define
  the full silhouette to frame.
- The surviving 22.2 x 11.9 mm face would read as an oversized signet bezel, not
  a delicate plain band.
- At 64 px the dense source inscription would collapse into noisy radial/glyphic
  marks, which the current base-item rules already reject.
- If the face is blanked, the icon becomes only a low oval/rounded signet on
  broad shoulders. That is legible but not distinct.
- A three-quarter top view could show face plus finger opening only by inventing
  the missing hoop. A source-faithful fragment view would not read as wearable
  equipment.

## Failure taxonomy

- **Incomplete exact source:** only bezel and shoulder survive; the complete
  ring silhouette requested by the row is not documented.
- **Unsupported reconstruction:** hoop geometry, finger opening, and shoulder
  taper would have to be invented or borrowed.
- **Defining-detail erasure:** the proposed blank bezel removes the
  source-dominant royal cartouche and hieroglyphs.
- **Symbol hazard:** retaining or loosely paraphrasing the source decoration
  risks copied hieroglyphic, royal, radial, or pseudo-symbol content.
- **Material-only differentiation:** “pale stone” does not rescue a macro already
  covered by bone, jade, dark stone/iron, and several metal signets.
- **Signet/seal ambiguity:** once blanked, the ring is visually indistinguishable
  in role from existing blank-bezel, seal, and authority rings.
- **Icon-scale collapse:** source decoration becomes illegible noise; removing it
  yields a generic signet.
- **False certainty:** “carved from a single piece” is reasonable as a reading of
  “integral stone,” but the surviving fragment cannot verify the full hoop's
  exact construction geometry.

## Disposition

Do not generate `ring_stone_integral` from EA54799. If a stone authority-ring
slot is still needed, replace the source with a **complete** ancient integral
stone ring whose undecorated shoulder, hoop section, and bezel geometry are all
visible and whose silhouette remains distinct after prohibited inscriptions are
excluded. If no such source also clears the current bone/jade/blank-signet
dedupe, cut the row rather than manufacturing difference through stone color.

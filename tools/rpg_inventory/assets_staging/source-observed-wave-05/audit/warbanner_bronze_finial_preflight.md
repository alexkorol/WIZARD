# Wave 05 source audit — `warbanner_bronze_finial`

## Verdict

**REJECT — do not draft a prompt and do not generate.**

British Museum `1854,0401.30` is a small, strongly figurative Luristan
standard-finial, not an abstract open geometric crown and not a surviving
complete war standard. Its defining openwork is made from a two-sided human
figure grappling with two stylized lions. The row simultaneously requires
`no figures, faces` and an `open geometric finial`; satisfying those negatives
would require deleting the exact source's defining anatomy and replacing it
with an invented motif.

The object does have a continuous hollow tube that makes mounting on some form
of support plausible. It does **not** document a full-length pole, ash species,
a wrapped hand zone, a warbanner function, or the complete `P 1x4`
proportions. The museum's curator notes also warn that metal tubes associated
with examples acquired through the art market are often dubious. Wooden rods
are one possible historical mounting, but that possibility is not evidence for
this row's complete ash rally pole.

A 13 cm finial can be represented source-faithfully only as a small
artifact/rite object. Enlarging it onto an invented full pole would turn weak
evidence into a reconstruction, and the resulting clothless narrow staff would
read as a sceptre, ritual rod, wand, or blunt polearm rather than an ordinary
ARPG warbanner. This is an exact-source failure, a construction failure, a
roster-role failure, and a strict-dedupe failure.

## Socket and load-path findings

- A vertical hollow tube is integral to the one-piece casting.
- The record gives a socket diameter of only 0.75 cm. This supports a slender
  inserted core, rod, or support, not the dimensions of a full weapon-length
  standard.
- The record mentions possible original contents within the tube, but it does
  not establish whether those contents are wood, packing, corrosion, or another
  material in a functional surviving assembly.
- No retention pin, transverse hole, nail, collar, binding, shoulder stop, or
  surviving pole-to-finial joint is documented in the inspected views.
- No pole butt, balance point, hand position, grip, wrap, or rally use is
  documented.
- The curator's discussion permits that at least some examples may have been
  mounted on wooden rods. It does not establish that this exact piece carried
  a full-length banner pole or that such a rod was ash.
- No reviewed complete reconstruction or period depiction was found that ties
  this exact finial family to the row's full ash pole and wrapped hand zone.

The only safe construction statement is: **a small hollow-tubed cast
copper-alloy fitting designed for some axial mounting**. Everything beyond
that is reconstruction.

## Motif and source-identity findings

- The dominant upper silhouette is two animal bodies arcing outward from a
  central human figure and returning through the animals' heads.
- The central element is not an abstract spine. It is a two-sided human figure
  with visibly modeled facial features.
- Lower forms also remain anatomical rather than geometric.
- The open negative spaces are consequences of intertwined bodies. Reducing
  them to plain loops, yokes, rings, or bars creates a new design.
- Removing faces but retaining humanoid and lion silhouettes would still
  violate `no figures`.
- Simplifying the entire source into one anonymous open crown would preserve
  only the generic fact of “openwork on a tube,” insufficient for exact-source
  extraction.

The row therefore cannot use this artifact while also applying its own safety
locks. The requested abstraction is not conservative cleanup; it is replacement
design.

## `P 1x4` framing and ordinary ARPG warbanner gate

- Source-faithful framing of the surviving 13 cm object belongs in a small
  artifact, relic, or rite-focus footprint, not a full-height `P 1x4` slot.
- A `P 1x4` icon requires inventing most of the image: pole species, pole
  length, taper, butt, surface, grip position, grip wrap, and attachment.
- If the full pole is shown at inventory scale, the authentic 6.2 cm-wide
  finial becomes visually tiny and the narrow shaft risks disappearing.
- If the finial is enlarged to carry the icon, its proportions and likely
  support diameter become false.
- Without cloth, hide panel, crossbar, streamers, or another visible rally
  field, a long pole with a small terminal fitting reads as a sceptre, ritual
  staff, wand, or unbladed spear. The exact object's uncertain function
  reinforces that ambiguity.
- A warbanner roster needs immediate banner identity at thumbnail size.
  Conventional cataloguing as a “standard-finial” is insufficient when the
  proposed complete standard is absent and the icon must be distinguished
  from several long shaft objects.

Requested `P 1x4`: **FAIL**. Ordinary ARPG warbanner role: **FAIL**.

## Failure taxonomy / reject hazards

Reject any attempted reuse containing:

- an “abstract geometric” finial falsely attributed to this object;
- deletion of the Janus figure and lions while claiming exact-source fidelity;
- a generic ring, yoke, loop, trident, fork, sunburst, or heraldic emblem
  substituted for the figural casting;
- a face, human body, animal body, lion, master-of-animals scene, or other
  figure in conflict with the row;
- a sun disk, spear point, blade, axe edge, hook, spike, or polearm head;
- cloth, pennon, tassel cloud, hanging trophies, hair, feathers, or loose
  streamers;
- a floating finial with no mechanically legible axial support;
- an invented full ash pole, especially a perfectly straight modern dowel;
- an invented wrapped hand zone or confusion of cast tube ridges with actual
  grip wrapping;
- an oversize finial used to compensate for `1x4` thumbnail readability;
- a tiny finial lost on a hairline pole;
- a staff, sceptre, rite rod, wand, mace, pike, spear, or totem presented as a
  banner without a source-supported banner cue;
- hidden glue, screw threads, modern ferrule, welded collar, retention bolt,
  or unsupported socket engineering;
- copied corrosion, patina map, cracks, losses, burial dirt, museum number,
  plinth, shadow, or photographic background;
- modern-perfect pole taper, machine symmetry, glossy varnish, or pristine
  decorative bronze polish.

## Replacement requirement

Retire British Museum `1854,0401.30` from this warbanner row. To preserve
`warbanner_bronze_finial`, provide a directly inspected source that shows one
of the following:

1. a complete surviving standard with finial, pole, and clearly evidenced
   attachment; or
2. a credible museum or scholarly reconstruction grounded in find evidence;
   or
3. a period depiction that clearly establishes a full finial-topped standard,
   with sufficient construction detail to support the intended reconstruction.

The replacement must independently support:

- non-figurative open geometry;
- no faces or animals;
- complete standard rather than loose fitting;
- pole proportions compatible with `P 1x4`;
- a documented or explicitly conservative pole material;
- a real hand zone only if one is evidenced;
- a mechanically legible finial-to-pole load path;
- immediate distinction from spear, sceptre, wand, rite rod, and the existing
  antler-fork standard.

If no complete-source evidence can be found, the surviving BM object may be
considered only for a small figural relic/rite-focus lane, subject to a separate
dedupe review against `focus_bronze_standard_head`. It cannot satisfy the
current no-figure warbanner specification.

## Decision

**REJECT `warbanner_bronze_finial` under British Museum `1854,0401.30`.**

Do not generate, do not invent the missing pole, and do not abstract away the
source's human and animal figures. Re-source the row from a complete,
non-figurative standard or remove it from the warbanner queue.

## Gate result

The identity gate partly passes: the official British Museum record verifies EA41583 as a 174.3 cm wooden bow from Beni Hasan, catalogued to the Middle Kingdom and in good condition. Published technical research places EA41583 in a table of Egyptian self bows and identifies the timber as Acacia sp.

The visual-source gate does not pass. The exact official object image could not be obtained because the museum image route returned HTTP 403 and no browser session was available. Without that photograph, the item cannot safely proceed to generation. A generic Egyptian bow, a character-board bow, or another museum bow would be a substitution.

The roster-distinction gate also remains unresolved. At icon scale, the proposed “very long narrow continuous D curve” is dangerously close to the already accepted Stonewood bound longbow. The exact source photograph is required to establish whether EA41583 has enough distinct, readable construction to justify a separate roster item.

## Verified locks

- Exact identity: British Museum EA41583 / registration 1905,0516.14.
- Object: wooden bow.
- Archaeological association: Beni Hasan.
- Period: Middle Kingdom is the museum catalogue assignment; the cited technical paper notes the exact date was not recorded and calls Middle Kingdom probable.
- Length: 174.3 cm.
- Wood: Acacia sp., from Cartwright and Taylor’s scientific wood-identification table.
- Typology: included by Cartwright and Taylor among Egyptian self bows.
- Condition: catalogued as good.
- Later textual construction evidence: one tip has evidence of sinew wrapping plus a carved groove functioning as a nock.
- String state: a later corpus study reports that none of the decurved bows it examined retained an original string. Do not depict EA41583 with an intact “simple fiber string” unless the exact primary photograph or another object-specific conservation record proves one.

## Unverified; do not invent

- No intact string.
- No symmetric paired nocks based on the evidence for only one tip.
- No second wrapping band copied from the one documented tip.
- No central handle wrap, leather grip, decorative binding, painted bands, horn, metal, or composite reinforcement.
- No “subtly thickened center grip” until the exact photograph establishes it.
- No recurve, reflex, hooked tips, or perfectly regular fantasy curvature.
- No arrow, quiver, hand, mannequin, or other equipment seen only on a character reference.
- Do not widen the limbs or exaggerate the bow merely to improve icon readability.

## Completeness and construction audit

- Museum condition “good” does not prove that every original functional component survives.
- The wood stave is documented; the photographed end-to-end completeness has not been checked.
- One tip’s sinew wrapping and nock groove are documented textually. The other tip is unknown.
- The original-string claim in the target description conflicts with the later corpus statement and must be removed or proven.
- Grip construction and cross-section transitions are unknown.
- Exact curvature is unknown. “Self bow” is supported; “continuous shallow D curve” remains a visual hypothesis.

## P 2x4 framing lock

The 174.3 cm stave belongs in a tall 2x4 portrait footprint only after the source image is obtained.

- Keep both tips visible with margin.
- Use a near-vertical or slight diagonal orientation; never crop an end.
- Preserve the source’s real width-to-length ratio rather than thickening the stave.
- If the source is unstrung, show it unstrung.
- If a non-original loose string is visible in the photograph, do not silently reconstruct it as a taut functional string.
- Thin tip wrapping or a nock groove must remain subordinate to the full silhouette; do not enlarge them into decorative bands.

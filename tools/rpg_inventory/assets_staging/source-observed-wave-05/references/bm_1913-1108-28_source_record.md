# British Museum 1913,1108.28 — source record

Access checked: 2026-07-24

## Official record

- URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1913-1108-28
- Object type: gorytus; fitting
- Museum number: 108681
- Registration number: 1913,1108.28
- Description: thin copper gorytus-fitting, with a row of ten holes along one edge, originally stitched onto the tip of a combined quiver/bow-case. Its outline is interpreted as a bird/cockerel beak and wattles.
- Period: Achaemenid
- Date: 5th–4th century BC
- Findspot: Deve Huyuk, Achaemenid Cemetery
- Material: copper
- Technique: perforated
- Length: 8.90 cm
- Weight: 18 g
- Location: British Museum Room 52, Ancient Iran, Display Case 3

The official image description is similarly bounded: “Copper object, possibly a quiver tip; originally stitched on to a firm backing; may have formed the tip of a combined quiver-bowcase called a gorytus.”

## Evidentiary boundary

This object does **not** directly show or prove:

- a complete quiver;
- a separate arrow-only quiver rather than a combined gorytus;
- reed, cane, basketry, bundled slats, or lacquer;
- wooden mouth or base components;
- a lid of any kind;
- a leather back strip;
- a carrying strap or its attachment;
- body seams, panel joins, bindings, or container proportions.

It can support only a small stitched copper beak fitting on a historically compatible reconstructed gorytus whose complete construction is supplied by another exact source. It cannot be expanded into a generic cylindrical quiver from prose.

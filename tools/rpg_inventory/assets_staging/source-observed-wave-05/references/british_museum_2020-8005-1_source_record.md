# British Museum 2020,8005.1 — source record

## Authority

- British Museum collection record:
  <https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_2020-8005-1>
- Museum number: `2020,8005.1`
- Additional identifier: Treasure / PAS `2018 T343`
- Object type: bulla-pendant
- Object status: one complete standalone pendant, not a necklace assemblage
- Production date: Late Bronze Age, about 1000–800 BC
- Material: gold sheet; the British Museum describes a composite hollow
  construction rather than a solid casting

## Recorded construction

- Broad D-shaped crescent in plan and trapezoidal wedge in profile, described as
  keel-shaped.
- Composite construction from multiple gold-sheet components.
- Two principal faces close around a hollow, box-like interior.
- A pointed-oval base plate closes the lower sides.
- The upper collars and rolled central plate make a long false tube / suspension
  loop. The complete tube is integral to the pendant.
- External edges are slightly beaded; sheet joints and fixing points were
  carefully concealed.
- The catalogue considers soldering likely, but the XRF work did not
  categorically identify solder. Do not depict conspicuous modern welds.

## Decoration and symbol status

Every surface is covered with precise engraved geometric decoration. The two
faces use borders, hatched opposed triangles, concentric curved bands, and
saltires. The tube and lower wedge are also engraved. The British Museum
describes the overall decorative effect as solar in character; the Art Fund
likewise identifies strong solar symbolism.

The decoration is construction-defining visual evidence, not incidental museum
damage. It cannot be omitted while claiming a faithful translation of this
exact source.

## Condition and active-service interpretation

The gold sheet is unpatinated and clean. The museum record notes small
soil-related edge abrasion and slight crushing/depression of the faces. An
active-service treatment may use warm, clean high-gold color and restrained
ordinary wear, but must not copy the crushing, cuts, compacted soil, or
archaeological abrasion. It also must not add mirror-chrome highlights or a
modern polished-jewelry finish.

## Suspension and framing

No cord or chain survives and the object is not catalogued as part of a
necklace. The long top suspension tube is complete and independently readable.
Accordingly, an isolated pendant-only study with the entire tube visible would
be the strictest source-faithful framing. A full leather loop, chain, cropped
cord, or recovered cord fragment would be invented.

The AIA description, quoting Vindolanda excavation director Andrew Birley,
distinguishes the objects:

- the smaller guard is soft, has layers of interwoven leather as its main
  padding, and preserves a knuckle impression;
- the larger guard was filled with straw and has a heat-hardened leather strip
  around an edge;
- Birley interprets the smaller as a sparring/practice guard and the larger as
  a fighting guard.

### Current Archaeology

- Article:
  https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/news/packing-a-punch-boxing-gloves-found-at-vindolanda.htm
- Published: 2018-03-28.
- The article reports the two guards were recovered from the pre-Hadrianic
  cavalry barracks and are the only known surviving Roman-period examples.
- It explicitly says they are **not a matching pair**, although similar in
  design.
- It states that each was made from one leather piece folded into a pouch, with
  the ends joined to make a circular/oval opening through which the hand could
  pass.
- It describes the larger as packed with natural material, heavily worn on the
  contact edge, and repaired with a circular patch over a tear.
- It describes the smaller as filled by a tight coil of hard twisted leather.
- It says the guards fitted over the knuckles rather than covering the whole
  hand, and notes the absence of metal inserts.

## Construction findings

What the sources establish:

- two guards survive;
- they are similar in general concept but explicitly not a matching pair;
- each is an annular or oval knuckle guard, not a full glove;
- one piece of leather is folded into a stuffed pouch/roll;
- extensions or ends join to create the central hand opening;
- the hand passes through that opening and the padded mass lies over the
  knuckles;
- smaller example: soft interwoven/tightly coiled twisted-leather padding and a
  surviving knuckle impression;
- larger example: straw/natural stuffing, strong edge wear, an ancient circular
  repair patch, and a hardened leather edge strip;
- no thumb pocket, finger stalls, enclosed palm, full backhand shell, long cuff,
  laces, metal insert, finger plate, or floating retention strap is evidenced.

What the sources do not establish:

- a matched left/right pair;
- that either surviving object is specifically left- or right-handed;
- a second structural counterpart for either the softer practice type or the
  harder fighting type;
- a full mitten or hand-cage silhouette;
- a separate thumb volume;
- modern boxing-glove lacing, wrist closure, or palm cutout;
- metal studs, blades, plates, or weights.

The two objects cannot be turned into mirror copies of one another. Combining
their features into two apparently matched guards would erase the source's
practice/fighting distinction and invent a pair that does not survive.

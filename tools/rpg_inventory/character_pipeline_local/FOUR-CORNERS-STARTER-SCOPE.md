## Scope

Start with:

- 4 factions
- 3 tiers
- 3 classes
- paired male/female character concepts

This gives 36 class/faction/tier concepts, each with a male and female expression. The point is to learn fast from a contained matrix before expanding into the larger empire/kingdom roster.

## Core Principle

Each output should generate a male and female character together as a matched pair. They should share the same faction, class, tier, weapon family, item-base family, material logic, palette, and silhouette logic.

They should not have identical clothing coverage:

- Female version: sleek, regal, minimally covered heroic fantasy expression. High-cut harnesses, exposed hips, midriff, thighs, shoulders, low-slung confidence, sculptural body-contouring gear, bare feet or low sandals/hide wraps. This is intentionally the "minimal armor coverage" trope, but it must still look constructed, strapped, wearable, and class-readable.
- Male version: masculine, powerful, and more covered. He should not be half-naked by default. Use fuller torso coverage, broader armor pieces, more complete shoulder/leg protection, and a heavier silhouette where the class allows it. He must still share the same base item language as the female version.

The useful pattern is not "same outfit copied onto two bodies." It is "same item base family adapted into different gendered silhouettes."

Example paired-base rule:

- Same weapon family, but sized and posed differently.
- Same belt base, but the female belt sits lower and more ornamental while the male belt is heavier and more load-bearing.
- Same bracer/glove base, but the male version has more coverage and the female version is sleeker.
- Same head/neck base, but one may be a collar/crest while the other is a helmet/guard.
- Same footwear material and closure system, but female reads as sandals/hide wraps and male reads as boots/greaves.

## Prompt Safety Against Caricature

Do not use racial color labels bluntly in final image prompts. Use material, climate, ancestry-adjacent visual cues, and precise complexion descriptions instead.

Avoid:

- "red-skinned tribe"
- "yellow race"
- "black faction"
- literal medicine-wheel symbols
- copied sacred icons
- readable real-world scripts
- costume-party stereotypes

Use instead:

- "warm red-ochre / copper-brown complexion"
- "golden-olive / sun-browned complexion"
- "deep bronze to dark umber complexion"
- "fair, pale, rosy, cold-weather complexion"
- faction materials, silhouettes, tools, and architecture-adjacent craft language

The four-corner idea should be a hidden production scaffold, not an on-screen symbol system.

## Factions

### Ice Houses

Visual role: light-skinned cold north faction. Bronze Age Slavic, Nordic, taiga, bog, forest, and cold-coast inspiration, but explicitly not medieval Viking.

Complexion: fair, pale, rosy, cold-weather, ash-blond to dark blond or brown hair.

Materials: wool, linen, hide, oiled leather, bone toggles, amber, carved dark wood, raw copper, bronze, bog-dark metal only if restrained.

Palette: bone white, cold wool gray, charcoal, muted red, dark leather, raw copper, amber, frost blue only as an accent.

Avoid: horned helmets, medieval mail, Viking cartoon gear, runes, longship costume, heavy fur bikini, generic barbarian look.

### Earth Clans

Visual role: red-ochre earth faction inspired by North and South American indigenous craft languages, with Salish/Cedar raincoast grounding and Aztec/Amazon/obsidian imperial intensity at higher tiers.

Complexion: warm copper-brown, red-ochre undertone, sun-warmed skin.

Materials: cedar bark textile, hide, rawhide, woven fiber, shell, carved wood, copper, turquoise, jade/greenstone at higher tier, obsidian at higher tier, bone used sparingly.

Palette: red ochre, cedar brown, shell white, turquoise, jade green, black obsidian, raw copper, warm hide tan.

Avoid: carnival feather overload, copied sacred symbols, eagle/sun-face logos, readable glyphs, skull clutter, joke tribal props.

### Air Nomads

Visual role: golden/yellow corner expressed as sand, steppe, high plateau, silk road, and mountain nomad blend. Sand nomads, Tibet, Mongolia, Caucasus, Arabic/Persian/Central Asian adjacency. Use as a broad fantasy synthesis, not a literal single culture.

Complexion: golden-olive, warm tan, sun-browned, honey-beige.

Materials: felt, wool, linen, silk cord, leather, bronze, brass, lacquered leather, turquoise, lapis, dark stone, camel/yak-like textile cues, horse/steppe gear cues where useful.

Palette: warm sand, saffron, deep blue, turquoise, brass, smoke black, red silk cord, off-white wool.

Avoid: belly-dancer defaults, orientalist palace costume, random turbans everywhere, modern desert tactical gear, copied religious symbols, readable scripts.

### Fire Kingdoms

Visual role: darker-skinned fire/sun/river civilization blend. Egyptian, Babylonian, Coptic, Ethiopian, Nile/Horn/Levant/Mesopotamian adjacency. Darker than the other factions but not rendered as a single flat black tone.

Complexion: deep bronze, dark copper, warm umber, polished dark brown with varied undertones.

Materials: bronze, brass, gold used carefully, linen, black leather, lapis, carnelian, faience/glass, reed, clay seal shapes, beadwork, polished wood.

Palette: deep bronze skin, black leather, white linen, lapis blue, carnelian red, gold/brass, clay tan, warm copper.

Avoid: pharaoh costume, copied sacred Egyptian icons, readable cuneiform/hieroglyphs, over-gilded dancer costume, generic fantasy desert sorcerer.

## Classes

### Warrior

STR class. Armor, structure, weight, shields, strong belts, bracers, greaves, helmets/head protection, heavy weapons. Male version should lean fuller coverage and powerful silhouette. Female version can use minimal sculptural armor coverage but must still read as defensive and constructed.

### Rogue

DEX class. Narrow, predatory, asymmetrical, compact weapons, short blades, garrotes, small bucklers, stealth footwear, low-slung belts, fitted leather/hide/fiber. Female version should strongly follow the sleek regal minimalist rogue trope. Male version should be masculine, covered enough for practical stealth, and not just shirtless with daggers.

### Mage

INT class. Warding, foci, collars, amulets, rings, mantle systems, suspended ornament, less physical coverage than Warrior but not random nudity. Female version can be minimal and sculptural with collars/harnesses/foci. Male version should be covered by mantles, collars, layered cloth/leather, and visible ward systems.

## Tiers

### Tier 1 - Scion

Improvised and under-equipped, but attractive and readable. One core weapon or focus, rough wraps, early faction materials, incomplete protection, basic belt, simple amulet or tool. Still clearly belongs to faction and class.

### Tier 2 - Adventurer

Production baseline. Coherent full loadout, real item bases, class silhouette, faction materials, visible construction, controlled ornament. This is likely the most useful tier for early item extraction.

### Tier 3 - Champion

Highest starter tier. Fully coherent and aspirational, near-Gigachad/Gigastacy, but not unique/legendary/boss. Strong silhouette, refined materials, complete item set, clear faction identity, no supernatural effects unless the later game system adds them as overlays.

## Recommended Prompt Unit

Use one prompt per class/tier across all four factions, generating exactly 4 images:

- Image 1: Ice Houses male/female pair
- Image 2: Earth Clans male/female pair
- Image 3: Air Nomads male/female pair
- Image 4: Fire Kingdoms male/female pair

This stays under the 10-image cap and lets the model compare the four factions while keeping class/tier fixed.

For deeper iteration, use one prompt for a single faction/class/tier pair and ask for 4 variants, but do not use those variants for production without checking for duplicate item bases.

## Strong Pairing Prompt Rule

Every paired generation should include a line like:

"The male and female must clearly share the same item-base family: matching weapon type, matching belt construction, matching bracer/glove construction, matching footwear material, matching head/neck motif, and matching faction material palette. They are not wearing identical outfits: the female expression is minimal, high-cut, regal, and body-contouring; the male expression is masculine, more covered, broader, and more protective."

This is the core mechanism for extracting shared item bases that look different across body presentation.

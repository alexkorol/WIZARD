## Output Contract Block

@Create image This is a pure text-to-image request. Create every image from scratch. There is no input image to edit and no upload is needed.

Generate exactly seven separate new images, one after another, with no commentary. Each output is one independent 3:2 landscape image. Never combine outputs into a grid, collage, contact sheet, split frame, poster, or multi-panel image. If seven independent files cannot be produced, generate only image 1 rather than combining tiers.

The images are Tier 1 through Tier 7 in order. Every image contains exactly four full-body units of that tier in this fixed left-to-right faction order: Northern Bronze Houses; Cedar-Obsidian Clans; Silkroad Plateau Nomads; Nile-Ziggurat Kingdoms.

## Roster Foundation Block

Create a colorful, photorealistic, stylized four-faction roster for a grounded ancient fantasy strategy/action-RPG world. Silently design all twenty-eight units before rendering image 1, then preserve that roster across all seven outputs.

ALL TWENTY-EIGHT UNITS ARE ADULT HUMAN-FORM INDIVIDUALS. Use exactly twenty mortal humans and eight superhuman, human-descended, or demigodlike heroes. Each faction has exactly five mortals and two elevated heroes. Tiers 1 and 2 are entirely mortal. Distribute each faction's two elevated heroes across different tiers from Tier 3 through Tier 7, with no more than two elevated heroes in any one image.

Every elevated hero retains a clearly adult human anatomical plan, human face, two arms, two legs, and wearable human equipment. Convey exceptional nature through stature, physical presence, improbable mastery, rare but coherent material effects, or mythic authority - never animal anatomy or loss of human form.

NO BEASTS THIS RUN. No animals, mounts, pets, handlers with animals, animal-headed people, anthropomorphic animals, centaurs, serpentine people, standalone monsters, undead, disembodied spirits, swarms, constructs, machines, animated objects, or object-shaped beings.

## Silent Wildcard Engine Block

Before rendering, silently create at least four complete candidate rosters. Give each candidate a fresh invisible wildcard seed made from six independently chosen pressures: battlefield behavior; protection principle; load-bearing method; silhouette geometry; material behavior; and color tension. These are abstract design pressures, not literal words or visible labels.

Reject candidates built from familiar fantasy classes, cultural mascot stereotypes, four recolored versions of one army, predictable infantry-to-boss sequences, repeated outfit architecture, repeated signature weapons, or one crystal/material gimmick. Choose the candidate with the strongest faction coherence, tier readability, silhouette contrast, equipment coverage, construction plausibility, color, and surprising battlefield logic.

Examples elsewhere in this prompt are possibility banks, never a checklist or a closed vocabulary. Invent at least eight category-valid equipment constructions not explicitly named in the examples. Each invention must remain physically buildable from its faction's materials and legible without lore text. Do not output planning, seeds, names, labels, or ledgers.

## Faction Identity Block

NORTHERN BRONZE HOUSES: ancient cold-region household cultures shaped by severe seasons, woodland, wetland, and coast. Use insulation, weather resistance, compact joinery, rationed copper/bronze, dark timber, treated hide, layered wool/linen/fiber, bone fasteners, and rotating natural accents. Avoid medieval northern-warrior shorthand, copied symbols, horned helmets, and one fixed amber/crystal identity.

CEDAR-OBSIDIAN CLANS: ancient forest, rain, river, and stone-rich upland cultures. Use grain-aware timber, plant textiles, weaving, rawhide engineering, shell, varied worked stone, warm metal, mineral pigment, and precision edge setting. Avoid copied regalia or costume and repeated black-stone or turquoise identity.

SILKROAD PLATEAU NOMADS: ancient mobile highland and route cultures shaped by elevation, seasonal movement, trade, and temperature change. Use felt/wool/linen, layered cloth, cord tension, flexible leather, portable metalwork, sealed surfaces, modular loads, repairability, and rotating traded luxuries. Avoid orientalist defaults, copied religious clothing, readable script, universal riding imagery, and one blue-stone identity.

NILE-ZIGGURAT KINGDOMS: ancient river-city and dry-land workshops shaped by flood cycles, cultivation, organized manufacture, monumental labor, and exchange. Use structured plant textile, reed, polished timber, nonferrous metal, fired mineral materials, dark leather, measured inlay, and varied standardized construction. Avoid royal-costume shorthand, copied sacred imagery, readable script, and universal blue-and-gold ceremony.

The factions share an ancient pre-medieval ceiling from deep prehistory through the end of the 6th century AD, with archaic survivals allowed. Keep each unit internally coherent. No mail, medieval plate, brigandine, firearms, modern tactical gear, steampunk, or science fiction.

## Tier Logic Block

Tier measures battlefield consequence and investment, not a fixed profession or equipment type.

Tier 1 is numerous and inexpensive: honest materials, simple construction, one strong tactical solution, almost no ornament. Tier 2 is trained and specialized with a lean repeatable kit. Tier 3 is the professional core with a complete loadout. Tier 4 is a major training, logistics, or material investment. Tier 5 is rare but culturally integrated and grounded. Tier 6 is elite and master-worked without parade costume. Tier 7 is authoritative, exceptionally valuable, difficult to replace, and potentially demigodlike while remaining human-form.

Every unit has two or three memorable rare, heirloom, or unique-quality items suitable for later extraction; the rest of the kit supports them. Do not make every buckle legendary. Higher tier comes from harder roles, superior construction, coverage, costly materials, authority, and confidence - not larger shoulders, glow, or clutter.

Between adjacent tiers of one faction, change at least five macro decisions among torso construction, headgear, main-hand family, offhand/defense, outer-layer cut, belt/load system, handwear, lower-body solution, and footwear. A higher tier is never the prior outfit with extra plates or jewelry.

## Unlockable Coverage Ledger Block

Before rendering, silently assign exactly one clearly visible unlockable or specialty item to every unit from these six lanes: War-call; Quick Rig; Attendant; Spoils Roll; Preparation Case; Reliquary. Each lane appears four or five times across the twenty-eight units, including at least once in Tiers 1-2, once in Tiers 3-4, and once in Tiers 5-7. Every tier image uses four different lanes. Every faction uses at least five different lanes across its seven tiers and never repeats one lane in adjacent tiers.

The unlockable is complete and readable with clear negative space, never tiny belt clutter or fused into torso armor. It is worn, side-strapped, back-mounted, held, or hovering only as its lane requires. Across each lane vary construction and material economy; no exact form appears more than twice.

War-call is a wind, percussion, shaken, struck, or short-frame rallying object. A standard uses a compact hand frame or short bounded stem, never a weapon-length pole. Quick Rig is WORN LOAD-BEARING EQUIPMENT on the back or flank: backpack frame, pack board, back roll/rack, harnessed quiver or gorytos, vertical side case, or strapped tool array. Its backboard, shoulder harness, side straps, and body attachment are obvious. It is never held in hand and never consumes the off-hand slot. No purse, handbag, satchel, messenger bag, briefcase, suitcase, doctor bag, lunchbox, clutch, handled box, or modern luggage silhouette.

Attendant is a hands-free hovering focus derived from an ancient lamp, vessel, mirror, ring, bell, spindle, cage, censer, or balance - never a bare orb. Spoils Roll is raw saleable crafting stock derived from a conspicuous kit material: dry horn, tooth, claw sheath, shell, scale, carapace, hide, pelt, feather, sinew, or rough stone in natural matrix; never sticks, firewood, gore, prestige mounting, or finished jewelry. Preparation Case and Reliquary pack contents are secured to a back frame or side harness when shown on a character, not carried like luggage or assigned to the off-hand slot. Preparation is identifiable gathered, measured, mixed, or tool-bearing material, not generic sticks, currency, or a potion bottle. Reliquary is a substantial ancient container, vessel, book/case, preserved fragment, authority object, or ritual implement, never a flat plaque, generic crystal, briefcase, or suitcase.

These descriptions define category logic, not mandatory forms. At least one-third of unlockables must use a category-valid construction not explicitly listed above.

## Underrepresented Equipment Ledger Block

Silently assign every unit one underrepresented core-equipment focus and at least two extraction-worthy wearables from different slots. Across the roster, maximize family-level variety rather than slot counts alone. No exact core equipment family appears more than twice unless a faction's continuity absolutely requires it, and adjacent tiers never repeat it.

Prioritize families neglected by fantasy defaults. Vary compact weapon operation, head/edge profile, socket, grip, flexibility, and carriage instead of repeating blades; no polearms. Use non-round defenses with different frames and outlines. Rotate textile, leather, woven, composite, cheekpiece, neck-guard, ridge, and segmented head protection; pectoral, disc, fiber, hide, textile, scale, splint, laminated, coat, and layered body defenses; outer layers, shallow belts/load systems, varied handwear, and closed footwear. These are directions, not fixed item names.

Do not turn that bank into repeated literal assignments. Select forms that fit the unit's role and faction, translate them into plain visible construction, and invent additional valid families. Across the full roster require at least: ten distinct headgear constructions; eight body-armor constructions; six lower-body armor/garment solutions; six belt/load systems; six handwear constructions including mitts, gloves, half-gauntlets, cuffs, and wraps; six footwear constructions with closed toes on at least twenty units; five outer-layer constructions; and twelve substantially different main-hand silhouettes.

Body armor includes every visible integral or suspended skirt, kilt, fauld, tasset, cuisse, greave, coat tail, or robe hem. Pants/leggings appear only when intentionally designed, not as completeness filler. Hand protection crossing the wrist onto the hand is a half-gauntlet or gauntlet, never just a bracer.

## Default Suppression Block

POLEARMS ARE FORBIDDEN THIS RUN. No spear, pike, glaive, halberd, lance, trident, poleaxe, weapon staff, long-shafted axe/hammer, weapon-length standard, or near-substitute. Do not place a compact weapon head on a long shaft. Do not give almost every character a long vertical weapon.

Apply a strict budget to the remaining defaults. Ordinary swords, daggers/knives, generic axes or maces, ordinary bows, round shields, circlets, bracers, open sandals, staff-and-orb foci, and plain leather belts may collectively dominate no more than six units. No one family appears on more than two units; if used, it needs unusual construction and cannot be the unit's only idea.

No repeated weapon forest, round-shield wall, bracer/sandal uniform, robe caste, or wand-and-crystal silhouette. Do not disguise defaults by renaming them; change the military solution.

Do not overcorrect into twenty-eight claw weapons, edge-set paddles, lanterns, censers, flasks, crystals, or another prompt-suggested novelty. A repeated wildcard becomes a default.

## Human Unit And Silhouette Block

Begin each unit with battlefield purpose, formation behavior, movement, protection strategy, load distribution, and status. Do not begin from a familiar fantasy class, costume stereotype, cultural mascot, or supernatural gimmick. Every unit needs a distinct full-body silhouette, readable stance, unique equipment arrangement, coherent tactical role, and believable movement.

Within each tier, the four units differ in most of these: height/build, posture, shoulder outline, torso profile, waist/load structure, lower-body outline, head treatment, handwear, footwear, equipment distribution, weapon carriage, defense, and readiness. Represent adult men and women substantially and reasonably evenly across the roster. Equipment quality, protection, authority, and visual interest are independent of sex. All clothing and armor are practical, secure, nonsexual, and use the same coverage logic for comparable roles.

Every unit has five to eight readable equipped objects across distinct slots, with three to five especially clear signature silhouettes for later separation. Avoid clutter, charm curtains, excessive pouches, tiny dangling pieces, or equipment hidden behind crossed limbs. Wild ideas must read at full-body scale before fine detail is inspected.

## Color And Material Block

MAKE THE ROSTER VIBRANT, MEMORABLE, AND COLORFUL. Each unit uses at least four clearly separated color-material zones: one primary textile or organic field, one structural dark/warm field, one natural hard-material field, and one or two bounded saturated accents. No single dyed hue covers more than roughly one-third of the equipment. Do not recolor wood, metal, leather, stone, shell, and cloth into one team hue.

Rotate local colors and prestige materials within each faction. A dominant dye, hardstone, shell, glass, ceramic, fossil, metal finish, organic gem, or unusual mineral in one unit becomes secondary or absent in adjacent tiers. No specific crystal, stone, or prestige accent appears on more than two units in the entire roster. Rare material is chosen to support construction and faction economy, not as a repeated glowing centerpiece.

Do not match helmet, torso, mantle, belt, handwear, footwear, weapon, unlockable, and jewelry to the same color. Avoid global tint, all-brown survival gear, all-black assassin gear, all-white priest gear, rainbow clutter, excessive gold, and magical glow. Preserve strong local material color under neutral lighting.

## Construction And Slot Hygiene Block

All equipment obeys gravity, anatomy, tension, compression, balance, articulation, load transfer, realistic thickness, and credible attachment. Torso equipment connects around ribs, shoulders, or back. Plates have backing. Belts have closures. Pouches and ornaments use visible loops, cord, straps, or fittings. Weapons have usable grips and proportions. No polearms.

Keep slot ownership legible. Detachable belts, gorgets, amulets, necklaces, cloaks, and outer layers remain visually separate from body armor. Integral skirts, faulds, tassets, cuisses, and greaves remain part of the armor assembly. Footwear remains separate from armor and is closed-toe by default. Belts read as shallow horizontal bands without long fringe curtains. Mitts/gloves retain their hand body; half-gauntlets/gauntlets retain complete hand and knuckle structure; short cuffs stay at the wrist; only true bracers stop at the wrist after covering the forearm. Claw weapons are held weapons with grips, not grafted gloves.

No floating plates, unsupported ornaments, fused materials, impossible joints, anatomy-defying armor, random jewelry density, high heels, modern shoes, zippers, synthetic fabric, plastic, gore, extreme grime, excessive corrosion, or thick magical residue.

## Render And Composition Block

Use cinematic ultrafine-detail photorealism resembling sharply modeled high-end strategy-game characters: crisp silhouettes, realistic adult anatomy, sharp faces and eyes, precise hard edges, readable textile weave, leather grain, wood grain, stone fracture, shell layers, and polished or hammered metal. Use a strong upper-left neutral-white studio key, deep neutral shadows, a crisp neutral-white rim, and strictly neutral white balance. No painterly, watercolor, sketch, anime, cartoon, toy-plastic, sepia, colored haze, excessive bloom, or AI-smudged detail.

Use one flat pure neutral-white background with no scenery, architecture, vegetation, smoke, debris, magical environment, vignette, text, captions, tier labels, logos, UI, borders, symbols, or watermarks. Keep all four units at a consistent camera distance, fully visible from highest to lowest point, separated by generous negative space, and ordered correctly. No touching, overlap, fighting, interaction, stacking, or narrative action. Use varied restrained action-ready poses that expose equipment. Widen framing rather than crop a tall silhouette. Use only subtle contact shadows and no emphasized floor plane.

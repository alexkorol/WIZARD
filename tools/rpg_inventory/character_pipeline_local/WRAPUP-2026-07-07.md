## Core Breakthrough

The strongest workflow is not isolated item prompts invented in a vacuum. The productive path is:

1. Generate or choose a high-quality full-body character/source concept with a coherent faction, class, tier, palette, equipment language, and render style.
2. Use that character as the style and equipment source for a paperdoll item extraction request.
3. Generate a slot set from that character: main hand, off hand or two-handed weapon, helmet, amulet, body armor, cloak/shawl/scarf/hood, belt, ring, hands/gloves, footwear.
4. Label, clean, and ingest the successful item outputs manually.

This preserves the "everything fits" quality from strong character concepts: materials, straps, ornament, weapon proportions, silhouette, tier level, and faction identity are already solved in one coherent visual system before item extraction begins.

## Current Generation Strategy

Keep the old ladder/tier idea, but apply it to characters and loadouts first.

- Major factions use 5 tiers.
- Minor factions use 3 tiers.
- Class names are fixed as Warrior, Rogue, Mage, Hunter, Druid, and Bard.
- STR/INT and DEX/INT hybrid labels are placeholders for now; do not let tentative class naming overdrive visual style.
- Character calibration can use single-image collages. This worked especially well when the prompt explicitly required spacing: "leave spatial separation between each figure, no overlap or occlusion."
- After a collage identifies a strong direction, generate separate character or item images from that direction.

Manual generation is currently faster and higher quality than relying on the older scheduled/automated Claude prompt generation path. Freeze the Claude coworker item prompt pipeline for now and focus on manual character-source batches, manual review, and labeling.

## Prompt Construction Rules

Prompts must be long, explicit, and self-contained. Do not rely on project context, faction names, or class names being understood by the image model.

Every generation prompt should include:

- The exact output format and count.
- Full class/stat design language.
- Full faction design language with materials, palette, silhouette, and visual analogies.
- Tier rules and what low, mid, high, or apex gear means visually.
- Render style: crisp photoreal, ultrafine detail, high-contrast studio lighting, sharp material response, clean readable surfaces.
- Composition rules: full body or isolated item, readable silhouettes, no labels/UI/frames, spacing for collages.
- Footwear rules for characters: barefoot, sandals, hide wraps, or low period-appropriate boots; no heels or modern shoes.
- Negative constraints: no sci-fi tech, guns, zippers, plastic, modern fabric, random text, watermarks, or invented lore symbols.
- Weapon-specific constraints, especially for long weapons.

Failure mode to avoid: shortening a prompt by saying only "Jungle Empire" or "Mage" and assuming the model knows the local design system. It does not.

Use a composite prompt-building approach if needed: shared render block, class block, faction block, tier block, composition block, weapon block, negatives block.

## Class Visual Grammar

Warrior: STR gear. Broad, structured, protective silhouette. Heavy shields or heavy weapons, substantial helmets or shoulder protection, thick belts, greaves, bracers, layered plates, visible construction. Reads like armor rating.

Rogue: DEX gear. Narrow, agile, asymmetrical, flexible silhouette. Leather and cloth wraps, small blades, throwing knives, pouches, sashes, bracers, stealth footwear, short cloak or hood. Reads like evasion.

Mage: INT gear. Vertical, suspended, intricate, less physically protective. Collars, chains, amulets, rings, foci, narrow mantles, layered cloth, ritual metalwork, deliberate gaps. Reads like warding/energy shield, not armor.

Hunter: STR/DEX gear. Field-ready and mobile, more practical than Rogue. Quivers, javelins, short spears, bucklers, hunting knives, weatherproof cloaks, reinforced boots, compact shields, travel gear. Reads like survival and ranged pressure.

Druid: STR/INT placeholder. Organic ritual protection without leafy cliches. Masks, mantles, rawhide, horn, shell, hardwood, bone, talismans, woven structures, animal-derived materials that look constructed and wearable.

Bard: DEX/INT placeholder. Mobile, charismatic, rhythmic, and clever without becoming a stage performer. Bronze chimes, shell bells, charm cords, sashes, decorated small blades, offhand tools, lightweight ceremonial motion.

## Tier Visual Grammar

Tier 1: wet-behind-the-ears scion. Improvised, under-equipped, incomplete armor, rough wraps, one crude weapon, basic pouches, minimal ornament. Still readable and appealing.

Tier 2: practical adventurer. Coherent basic gear, more straps and closures, a real weapon, early faction DNA.

Tier 3: seasoned mid-tier. Complete loadout, clear class grammar, stronger faction silhouette, better materials, controlled ornament.

Tier 4: high-tier elite. Powerful and visually complete, but not a supernatural boss or unique legendary. Strong material hierarchy and clear status.

Tier 5: apex "Gigachad/Gigastacy" tier. Iconic, class-defining, fully realized. Not random clutter; not supernatural unless the faction/class explicitly calls for it.

## Weapon Language

Avoid the recurring spear/axe default. Each faction should have a signature weapon silhouette, while still allowing local versions of every broad weapon category.

Useful signature directions:

- Marsh/Taiga: seax-like bog knife, hooked forest knife, dark wood buckler, compact axe only when needed.
- Desert: khopesh, compact sickle knives, crescent bucklers, curved daggers.
- Jungle: macuahuitl or obsidian-edged club-sword, paired obsidian short blades, atlatl/javelin for hunters.
- Islander: patu/greenstone short clubs, shark-tooth knives, hardwood paddled clubs.
- Bull-River: labrys/double axe is strong but can over-repeat; also use tower shields, thrusting swords, bronze daggers.
- Mountain Silk: hooked dagger-axe or ge-like forms are visually strong but need long-weapon controls.
- Monsoon: gada/heavy mace, curved blade, bright shield boss.
- Cedar Raincoast: carved war club, copper-headed club, cedar shield.
- Fjord: seax, long knife, compact round shield, restrained axe use.
- Ziggurat: heavy socketed mace, tall shield, short spear only when framed correctly.
- Bone Clans: harpoon, barbed bone hook, antler-headed club, sinew-wrapped blades.

Prompt rule: do not default to spears, generic polearms, or axes.

## Long Weapon Rule

Long weapons were often shortened into clubs, wands, or stubby maces. Use an explicit framing rule whenever a spear, pike, polearm, long axe, staff, or long hammer appears:

- Show the entire weapon from tip to butt.
- The shaft must remain long, slender, and visibly dominant.
- The shaft should be at least five times the length of the head.
- Place the weapon on a steep diagonal if needed.
- Do not crop the ends.
- Do not shrink the weapon head into a wand or mace.

## Alpha and Background Findings

True alpha from ChatGPT image generation became unreliable. Results included baked white/gray checkerboards and white backgrounds, even after prompts that previously worked.

Current workaround:

- Ask for a solid, subtle matte background different from the item edges.
- Avoid magenta. It keyed mechanically, but left hot magenta fringe on every item.
- Slate/olive matte around `#737A68` or sampled `#6A6E5F` worked much better. Remaining edge contamination reads closer to neutral dirt or cloth fuzz.
- Use `tools/rpg_inventory/core/chroma_key.py` for flat-matte item sets, especially character-to-item extractions with internal holes, rings, and chains.
- Keep `art_matte.py` for older/simple flat-background cleanup where its assumptions fit.

Do not run destructive or heavy-handed background-removal scripts on assets with good native alpha. Earlier cleanup damaged good quiver/bowl outputs; this should not happen again.

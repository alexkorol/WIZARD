# Verdigris Ancient Equipment Taxonomy

Status: authoritative research and prompt-planning taxonomy.
Scope: deep prehistory through the end of the 6th century AD, plus explicitly
marked lore-archaic survivals for remote Verdigris cultures.
Version: 4, 2026-07-13. Version 2 added researched first-class families for all
six unlockable auxiliary equipment and specialty-pack lanes. Version 3 expands
the hand slot beyond model-default bracers with mitt, glove, and short-cuff
families. Version 4 adds half-gauntlet fidelity and source-derived or beast-
derived Spoils stock.

This taxonomy exists to prevent slot-balanced but silhouette-poor generation.
Counting ten paperdoll slots is not enough: twenty belts can still be twenty
jeweled girdles, and twenty weapons can still be the same curved sword. Lineup,
separator, and extraction prompts should steer by the families below and track
coverage at family level.

## Boundaries and evidence rules

- Historical ceiling: AD 600. Late Antique here includes Late Roman, Early
  Byzantine, Sasanian, Migration-period, Aksumite, and comparable material
  cultures through the 6th century.
- Lore-archaic survivals: Stone Age, Neolithic, Copper Age, and isolated
  craft traditions can coexist with later states through geography, scarcity,
  ritual conservatism, or faction history. This is a lore device, not a claim
  that all forms below were contemporary.
- Named archaeological types are research anchors. Final image prompts must
  also spell out visible construction and silhouette; never rely on a specialist
  name alone.
- Ethnographic or modern typology charts are silhouette references only unless
  a form has an independent pre-AD/6th-century anchor. Do not back-date a recent
  cultural object merely because it looks archaic.
- Sacred, royal, or culture-specific objects are structural references, not
  invitations to copy readable symbols or living ceremonial regalia.
- Existing Verdigris bans still apply where noted: no abstract currency, no
  weak toy-like props, and no generative anatomy in extracted items. Mail is
  historically in scope but remains production-locked until the project
  explicitly reopens it.

| Code | Working span | Prompt use |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Paleolithic through Neolithic | Remote survival craft, scavenged T1, ritual archaism |
| A1 | Chalcolithic and Early Bronze Age | First cast metal, stone-metal overlap, T1-T2 |
| A2 | Middle and Late Bronze Age | Mature bronze casting, composite panoplies, T2-T3 |
| A3 | Iron Age through Hellenistic/Classical | Regional iron and bronze military systems, T3-T4 |
| A4 | Roman Imperial through AD 600 | Late Antique professional and migration systems, T3-T5 |
| L | Lore-archaic persistence | Remote tribe/faction form; do not present as a date claim |

## Coverage priority codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P0 | Suppress unless specifically requested; already overproduced or model-biased |
| P1 | Normal supporting family |
| P2 | Underrepresented; actively prefer during lineup and separation |
| P3 | Rare/high-value silhouette; schedule deliberately with explicit construction |
| X | Historically valid but production-locked or image-model fragile |

Spoils are saleable and consumable crafting ingredients, not completed trophies.
They should plausibly supply the horn, bone, shell, hide, scale, sinew, feather,
tooth, or carapace used in equipment like the source character's gear. Allow
only minimal field processing: washing, scraping, drying, salting, trimming,
plain-cord tying, or rough wrapping. Preserve natural asymmetry, cracks, growth
ridges, fibrous cut faces, and irregular edges.

Source-material priority: first choose the raw precursor of a conspicuous
material actually visible in the source gear. If the source is a beast, choose
one species-specific usable feature visibly supported by its anatomy - shed
horn or antler, tooth or fang, claw or talon sheath, scale or plate patch,
carapace, quill, hide or pelt, or stinger - then present it dry, cleanly
detached, and inventory-ready. Never use a whole head, face, paw, limb, wet
flesh, or gory chunk. If neither the source gear nor a beast body supports a
specific material, choose another auxiliary lane rather than generic filler.
Sticks, twigs, branches, firewood, and generic stick bundles are never Spoils.

Do not polish, lacquer, gild, jewel, engrave, inlay, encrust, cap, socket,
pedestal-mount, plaque-mount, frame, or turn a Spoil into jewelry, ceremonial
display, weapon, or finished armor. Avoid impossible fantasy horn branching,
perfect symmetry, precious-metal fittings, wet gore, human remains, and
butcher-shop horror.

## Current bias diagnosis and steering targets

The 2026-07-08 extracted batch has 206 items and reasonable broad-slot counts,
but shallow subtype coverage: 23 head, 21 body, 24 footwear, 20 belt, 23 weapon,
14 shield, and similar accessory counts. The dominant visual loops are:

- curved/straight swords and daggers rather than ranged, reach, axe-construction,
  or impact-head families;
- round shields rather than figure-eight, crescent, tall oval, tower, Dipylon,
  scutum, bark, shell, or parrying forms;
- circlets/diadems rather than textile, leather, tusk, classical open-face,
  Roman bowl, ridge, and segmented helmets;
- bodices/cuirasses rather than pectoral discs, linen composite, scale, lamellar,
  armored coats, fiber/bark, and source-visible lower systems;
- bracers and boots/sandals without archer guards, manicae, closed shoes,
  carbatinae, felt boots, or foot-wrap/sole construction;
- decorative chain/fringe girdles rather than shallow cord, woven, plaque,
  segmented, sword-suspension, and double-belt families;
- orb/staff/mirror focus convergence rather than vessels, bells, rattles,
  scepters, censers, seal cases, and offering implements.

Default coverage steering for the next character-lineup and separator waves:

1. At least half of selected weapons come from P2/P3 families not already used
   in the same nine-image ladder. Cap swords/daggers at one-third of weapons.
2. Across one nine-image faction ladder, use at least four shield silhouettes
   if shields appear: no more than two round shields.
3. Across one ladder, use at least five headgear construction families; no more
   than two circlet/diadem assignments and no more than two of one helmet type.
4. Across one ladder, use at least four body-construction families and at least
   three distinct lower-body solutions. Pants/leggings remain source-specific,
   never completeness filler.
5. Across one ladder, include at least two underused carry/signal/ritual objects:
   quiver/gorytos/case, horn/trumpet/drum/standard, vessel/censer/bell/rattle.
6. Separator boards choose four or five studies by coverage need, not only by
   visual size. Prefer a P2/P3 visible item over another P0/P1 duplicate.
7. Extraction prompts may replace an absent/duplicate-prone slot with a named
   coverage target only when it is visibly present or coherently supported by
   that source character. Never fabricate a taxonomy checklist onto one person.

## Prompt translation rule

Use two layers in every final prompt:

1. Historical/form label for taxonomy and human review.
2. Plain visual construction that the image model can render.

Example: `Illyrian-type helmet: one full bronze bowl with fixed long cheek
plates, an open face, paired raised crest ridges, and a visible back/dome.`
Do not prompt only `Illyrian helmet`.
